I don't think you beat sadness. You carry the fucker.

When it's small, I work out. Lift something heavy. Go play pickleball. Watch the Canes. Play guitar. Write code. Do some push-ups just to prove to myself I'm still in the fight.

When it's bigger... I text my people. CW. CL. RB. TH. "The Dude's Group." The boys if it's about them. I don't hide it worth a damn. I've cried over Jacob and Jackson moving out. I've cried over dead friends. I've cried because the marriage felt like it was slipping through my fingers. I'm not embarrassed by any of that. I cry easily, especially when it comes to my sons and the people I love.

Sometimes I pour a bourbon. Sometimes that's a good idea. Sometimes it's absolutely not.

I also lean into the dumb little things that remind me life isn't *only* grief. Hockey. Wes Anderson movies. A stupid Shoresy quote. Making friends laugh with some completely juvenile bullshit. Dark humor isn't avoiding the sadness. It's making the sadness share the room with something else.

The biggest thing, though?

I quit pretending I'm "fine."

If somebody I trust asks, "You doing okay?" they're probably gonna get the truth. Maybe just, "Oof." Maybe a three-hundred-word 1 a.m. wall of text. Depends on the night.

Sadness doesn't mean you're broken. It usually means you gave a shit about someone or something. That's a pretty decent trade, even though it hurts like hell. Love just sends the bill later.
