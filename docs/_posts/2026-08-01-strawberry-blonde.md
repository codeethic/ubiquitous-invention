There are a handful of days in your life where, if you could go back and watch them from the outside, you'd spend the whole time screaming at yourself.

*"Pay attention, dumbass."*

The day I met Tamara is one of those.

It started as one of those completely unremarkable Raleigh nights. My good friends J & E, in an attempt to cheer me up, took me Sullivan's downtown. I was doing what I did in those days—trying random martinis, because apparently I thought I was sophisticated instead of a lump making questionable decisions.

One of those decisions was a thing called the Strawberry Blonde.

Holy fuck.

It was awful.

I couldn't drink it. Rather than let it go to waste, I asked E to walk it over to this woman sitting with another group and offer it to her.

Imagine introducing yourself to your future wife by saying, essentially, *"Here. You throw this away."*

Smooth.

Apparently she tried it.

Apparently she also thought it was awful.

So we're off to a phenomenal start.

Later that night her group headed over to a place next door. I inquired to the destination, probably assuming I'd never actually show up, she spilled the beans.

But I did show up, Fools!

I saw her across the room and immediately asked her to dance. At some point I asked her what her favorite movie was.

"Footloose."

Without hesitation, and with the confidence only an idiot in his mid-thirties possesses, I said, "I'll be your Kevin Bacon."

Reader...

I was not Kevin Bacon.

Not even close.

I have no idea why she kept talking to me after.

At the end of the night we exchanged numbers. I texted her after I was home. We talked on the phone for over an hour, and somewhere in that conversation we figured out we were both flying out the next morning.

Different destinations.

Same airport.

We made plans to grab breakfast before our flights.

Neither of us made breakfast because we were both running late. Very late.

Again... stellar execution.

Then life decided to intervene.

I got to my gate and there she was.

Same flight.

Not just the same airport.

The same damn plane.

My seat was directly behind hers, and I managed to swap so we could sit together. We talked the whole flight to Atlanta like we'd known each other forever.

It's funny how life works.

People spend years trying to optimize everything. Five-year plans. Career ladders. Apps with filters and compatibility scores and algorithms.

Meanwhile one of the biggest turning points in my life started because I ordered a shitty martini.

If I'd picked bourbon instead?

If E hadn't walked that drink over?

If either of us had actually made breakfast?

If one of us had missed that flight?

Who the hell knows.

I think about that sometimes.

Life isn't usually changed by grand gestures.

Most of the time it's changed by tiny, stupid, almost accidental decisions that don't look important until years later.

A bad cocktail.

A dance.

A terrible Kevin Bacon impression.

A seat assignment.

That's all it was.

Until it wasn't.
