title: "The Measure of My Son"
date: 2026-07-08
---

There are moments in parenting that announce themselves.

First steps.

First day of school.

The first time they drive away by themselves.

Then there's the day you carry the last box into a dorm room, hug your son goodbye, walk back to your car, and realize your house will never sound the same again.

That was Jackson.

I've spent a lot of my life thinking I was pretty tough.

Turns out that's complete bullshit when it comes to my boys.

The night before we moved Jackson into NC State, I sat down and wrote him a letter. I knew I wasn't going to be able to say everything I wanted without getting emotional. I figured I'd get the tears out ahead of time.

It helped.

A little.

I still cried.

I joke with my friends that any texts after three in the afternoon on move-in day were probably written after a couple beers.

That wasn't really a joke.

People think becoming an empty nester is freedom.

Maybe someday.

For me it just meant the two people I've loved most in this world no longer lived under my roof.

Jackson has always been different from his brother.

Jacob has always been relentlessly driven. You never have to wonder if he's going to put in the work. That's just who he is.

Jackson is wired differently.

That's probably the hardest part about being a parent.

You don't stop worrying because they turn eighteen.

You just lose the ability to protect them.

Parents always have one lesson they wish they'd taught better.

When I wrote that letter before college, I told him something I've never believed about anyone more than I believe about him.

He squeezes every ounce out of life.

Watching him become his own man has made me want to become a better father.

A better person.

He has exceeded every expectation I ever had for him.

And the truest sentence I've ever written is still this:

*You represent the greatest achievement of my life.*

Not my career.

Not serving in the Air Force.

Not becoming financially independent.

Not retiring early.

Him.

Both my boys.

Everything else is just noise.

The world feels heavy these days.

Politics.

Hatred.

People screaming at each other.

Some days it honestly feels like we're coming apart at the seams.

Then I think about Jackson.

I think about the man he's becoming.

I think about him building a life that's entirely his own.

And I remember that the future isn't some abstract idea.

The future has a face.

It's my son's.

I've spent fifty years learning how to explain software, markets, hockey, politics, and every other damn thing under the sun.

But when it comes to Jackson, none of those words really matter.

If you asked me what I'm proudest of, I'd have the answer before you finished the question.

My greatest achievement isn't something I built.

It's someone I helped raise.
